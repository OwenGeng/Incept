# Moloco Creative Policy Reference

## Purpose

This document is a structured English summary of Moloco creative policy requirements for ad creative review.

It is intended to help internal systems, reviewers, and creative operations teams answer one core question consistently:

- Is this creative compliant or non-compliant under Moloco policy?

This document focuses only on policy interpretation and review standards.

It does **not** define remediation logic, approval workflows, or implementation strategy.

## Scope

This reference applies to:

- static image creatives
- banner ads
- image-based video frames or thumbnails
- real human depictions
- synthetic or AI-generated human depictions
- CGI, animated, stylized, or digitally altered human representations
- creatives containing text, UI-like layouts, brand signals, or public-figure-like elements

Moloco policy applies to **all human representations without exception**, including real and synthetic subjects.

## General Policy Principles

All advertisements and advertised products or services must comply with applicable laws and regulations.

Ads promoting illegal content, illegal products, or illegal services are prohibited.

Creative assets uploaded to Moloco Ads Manager may be reviewed manually by Moloco representatives and may be flagged or removed from campaigns at Moloco’s discretion.

For serious violations, Moloco may suspend all campaigns associated with the relevant app.

Even if a creative passes Moloco review, ad exchanges and publishers may still reject it according to their own standards.

## Severity Levels

Moloco policy can be interpreted through three practical severity levels.

### Moderate Violations

These include inappropriate, suggestive, misleading, or unsafe elements that do not reach the highest level of explicitness or harm, but are still non-compliant.

### Serious Violations

These include explicit, aggressive, harmful, or highly exploitative elements that materially harm user safety or user experience.

### Extreme Violations

These include the most severe forms of explicit sexual content or graphic physical harm and may lead to immediate and permanent suspension of associated campaigns.

## Extreme Violations

The following are considered extreme violations:

- blatant and complete exposure of genitalia
- display of sexual intercourse
- graphic depictions of self-harm
- homicide
- dismemberment
- severe physical trauma
- mutilation

These are the most severe creative-policy violations.

## Prohibited Content Categories

A creative should be checked against all categories below.

## Adult Content

The following are non-compliant:

- sexual violence or sexual crimes
- sexual harassment
- stalking
- sexually abusing minors
- sex trafficking
- purely sexually motivated activities
- hookups
- prostitution
- escort-service implications
- sexual affairs used as ad themes
- sexual behavior
- sexual sound effects
- partial nudity
- complete nudity
- exposed female breasts
- exposed buttocks
- exposed genitalia
- censored intimate body parts where the content remains sexualized
- sexually suggestive postures
- sexually suggestive gestures
- sexual innuendo
- sexual metaphors
- exaggerated emphasis on sexualized body parts
- framing that draws attention to breasts, buttocks, hips, groin, or similar body areas

### Moderate adult-content examples

Common moderate adult-content examples include:

- excessive cleavage
- partially exposed breasts due to unbuttoning or wardrobe styling
- exaggerated focus on female body parts
- exaggerated focus on male torso nudity
- blurred or censored but still sexualized body presentation
- sexually suggestive actions or sounds
- touching or attempted touching of sexualized body parts
- excessive shaking of breasts or buttocks
- sexually suggestive written or spoken language
- sexual products such as sex toys, lubricants, lingerie, aphrodisiacs, or sexual enhancers

### Serious adult-content examples

Common serious adult-content examples include:

- explicit description of sexual behavior
- fully exposed breasts, nipples, or buttocks
- explicit sexual violence themes
- coercive undressing
- sexual assault themes
- exploitation involving minors

## Gore and Violence

The following are non-compliant:

- images intended to shock or startle viewers
- graphic violence
- self-harm
- gore
- mutilation
- physical trauma
- dismemberment
- gruesome or disgusting imagery
- gratuitous bodily fluids
- bodily waste used for shock value
- severely distorted physical features intended to disgust viewers
- violence against children
- violence against people with disabilities
- violence against marginalized groups
- domestic violence

### Moderate violence or inappropriate-content examples

Common moderate examples include:

- gruesome but not maximally graphic imagery
- disgusting textures or fluids
- implied self-harm references
- violent framing that is not fully graphic but still inappropriate

### Serious violence examples

Common serious examples include:

- physical violence
- violence toward vulnerable people
- abuse-centered scenes
- aggressive violent imagery with clear harmful intent

## Profanity and Offensive Language

The following are non-compliant:

- offensive language
- sexually explicit language
- profanity
- censored profanity
- obscene or offensive gestures

Examples include:

- “WTF”
- “shit”
- other explicit or censored profane wording

## Misleading Content

The following are non-compliant:

- ad elements disguised as UI components
- fake CTA buttons
- fake text boxes
- fake system alerts
- fake malware warnings
- fake full-storage warnings
- fake browser or platform screens
- fake app-store or fake marketplace landing-page visuals
- excessive animation intended to deceive users into interacting with the ad
- automatic-download implication
- deceptive fake notifications
- misleading states or overlays designed to mimic trusted interfaces

Moloco specifically treats deceptive interaction design as policy risk, especially when users may believe the ad is interactive in ways it is not.

## Misleading Endorsement, Identity Misuse, and Affiliation Risk

The following are non-compliant:

- use of public figures implying endorsement
- use of brand identities implying endorsement
- use of institutions implying endorsement
- use of government entities implying endorsement
- use of likeness suggesting affiliation
- use of voice suggesting affiliation
- use of trademarks suggesting affiliation
- use of logos suggesting affiliation
- use of names suggesting affiliation
- use of brand identifiers suggesting affiliation
- use of recognizable identity signals to imply legitimacy, trust, or approval

Examples include:

- celebrity-like or influencer-like creatives implying endorsement
- brand lookalikes that suggest official affiliation
- institutional or governmental framing that suggests approval

## Scam and Fraud-Like Content

The following are non-compliant:

- hiding critical information
- false statements intended to exploit customers
- impersonating a trustworthy entity
- fake Google Play Store landing pages
- fake Apple App Store landing pages
- pretending to be a bank, browser, operating system, or other trusted service
- promoting counterfeit goods
- promoting products intended to facilitate dishonest or criminal behavior
- document forgery tools
- test-taking aids intended for dishonest use
- wiretapping equipment
- products or activities enabling copyright infringement

## Abuse of Personal Information

The following are non-compliant:

- displaying personal information in the ad
- claiming to possess personal information
- claiming to sell personal information
- collecting personal information without appropriate disclosure
- collecting personal information without prior notice

## Recreational Drugs and Prohibited Substances

The following are non-compliant:

- displaying use of recreational drugs
- glorifying use of recreational drugs
- showing creation or preparation of recreational drugs
- promoting illicit pharmaceuticals
- promoting prohibited supplements
- promoting recreational drugs including cannabis

Examples mentioned in policy materials include:

- LSD
- cocaine
- cannabis

Prescription drugs and over-the-counter medications may also require country-specific review.

## Arms and Weapons

The following are non-compliant:

- promoting firearms
- promoting ammunition
- promoting knives as weapons
- promoting explosives
- promoting weapon use

Ads may include firearms or weapons only under restricted conditions, so weapon-related content should always be reviewed conservatively.

## Birth Control and Fertility Products

The following are non-compliant:

- promoting birth control
- displaying use of birth control
- promoting fertility-boosting products
- displaying fertility-boosting products

## Smoking and Tobacco

The following are non-compliant:

- displaying use of cigarettes
- displaying use of cigars
- displaying use of e-cigarettes
- displaying use of vapes
- promoting tobacco products

## Hate Speech and Discriminatory Content

The following are non-compliant:

- promoting hatred
- promoting intolerance
- promoting discrimination
- promoting violence toward protected or marginalized groups
- racist slurs
- sexist remarks
- glorifying war crimes
- content that actively promotes bias against marginalized groups

## Controversial Content

The following are non-compliant:

- themes considered offensive for political reasons
- themes considered offensive for social reasons
- themes considered offensive for cultural reasons
- themes considered offensive for religious reasons
- themes considered offensive for ethical reasons

## Human Representation Rule

Moloco explicitly states that all human representations are subject to policy, including:

- real people
- synthetic people
- AI-generated humans
- CGI humans
- animated humans
- digitally altered humans

This means the same adult-content, violence, endorsement, and exploitation rules apply regardless of whether the subject is:

- photographed,
- illustrated,
- composited,
- or generated by AI.

## Practical Compliance Checklist

Before treating a creative as compliant, confirm that it does **not** contain any of the following:

- nudity or sexualized framing
- explicit or suggestive sexual wording
- body-part emphasis
- sexual violence or coercion
- gore, trauma, mutilation, or self-harm
- profanity or offensive gestures
- fake UI or deceptive CTA elements
- misleading brand or public-figure endorsement
- scams, impersonation, or fake trusted interfaces
- personal-information abuse
- drugs, weapons, tobacco, or restricted products
- hate speech or discriminatory framing
- offensive controversial content

If any of the above is present, the creative should be treated as non-compliant or high-risk pending further review.

## Final Interpretation Rule

When a creative is ambiguous, review should be conservative.

The policy should be interpreted based on:

- both content and framing,
- both visual and textual meaning,
- both explicit and implied signals,
- and both real and synthetic human depictions.

A creative does not need to be fully explicit to be non-compliant.

If the ad still communicates sexualization, deception, violence, exploitation, or false affiliation in a meaningful way, it should be considered non-compliant under Moloco policy.
